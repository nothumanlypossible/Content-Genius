# Product Marketing Context

*Last updated: 2026-03-13*

---

## Product Overview

**One-liner:** Crabeezy is a dock-to-dish marketplace that connects Maryland watermen directly with buyers — no wholesaler, no middleman.

**What it does:** Crabeezy gives independent Chesapeake Bay watermen a mobile storefront to list fresh catch, set their own prices, and get paid instantly via Stripe. Buyers open the app, see a live map of fresh hauls and steamed-to-order listings near them, and buy direct. Every seller is verified through DNR commercial fishing licenses and health permits.

**Product category:** Local food marketplace / direct-from-producer app (buyers search for "fresh crabs near me," "local seafood," "buy direct from waterman")

**Product type:** Two-sided marketplace (mobile app)

**Business model:** Transaction fee on each sale. Watermen list and sell for free. Stripe handles instant payouts.

---

## Target Audience

### Supply Side — Watermen (Sellers)

**Who they are:** Independent commercial crabbers and watermen working the Chesapeake Bay and Maryland tidal waters. Hold DNR commercial crabbing licenses. Often family operations, generational trade. Work long hours (4am–noon on the water). Sell catch to wholesalers at suppressed rates because they have no direct-to-consumer channel.

**Geography:** Chesapeake Bay region — primarily Dorchester, Talbot, Anne Arundel, Calvert, St. Mary's, Baltimore, and Queen Anne's Counties.

**Age range:** 30s–60s. Strong Facebook users. Not early tech adopters but use smartphones daily.

**Jobs to be done:**
- Sell their catch at a fair price without waiting on a wholesaler check
- Reach buyers who will pay retail prices for fresh, local seafood
- Handle walk-up dock sales and pre-orders without cash-only chaos
- Build a repeat customer base without a middleman controlling the relationship

**Pain points:**
- Wholesaler prices leave money on the table — watermen see 15–20% less than they'd earn selling direct
- No easy way to let buyers know what's available today
- Walk-up dock sales are inconsistent and cash-only
- No digital presence or storefront to manage pre-orders

---

### Demand Side — Buyers

**Who they are:** Maryland and DMV residents who care about fresh, local seafood. They know the difference between a picked-yesterday crab and a warehouse crab. Many have fond memories of buying from the dock or from someone they know. They want that connection back but don't know how to find it reliably.

**Geography:** Maryland (Anne Arundel, Baltimore, Montgomery, Howard, Calvert, Prince George's Counties) + DC and Northern Virginia buyers willing to drive for quality.

**Age range:** 25–55. Heavy Facebook and Instagram users. Comfortable buying online.

**Jobs to be done:**
- Find fresh crabs and seafood without calling around or driving to docks blindly
- Know the catch is actually fresh and legally sold (not sketchy back-of-truck sales)
- Support local watermen and the Chesapeake Bay fishing tradition
- Get a better product than what the grocery store or chain seafood market offers

**Pain points:**
- No reliable way to find fresh, local catch — word of mouth only
- Can't tell if a dock is open or what's available before driving there
- Grocery store crabs and chain seafood are inferior and overpriced
- Feel disconnected from where their food comes from

## Problems & Pain Points

**Waterman core problem:** The wholesale model was built to benefit the intermediary, not the producer. Watermen are price-takers with no leverage and no direct relationship with their end customer.

**Buyer core problem:** Fresh, local seafood is available near them — they just can't find it. There's no discovery layer for dock-direct purchases.

**Why current solutions fall short:**
- **Wholesalers/retailers:** Take margin from the waterman and deliver inferior freshness to the buyer
- **Informal dock sales:** Inconsistent, cash-only, no way for buyers to know when and where to show up
- **Facebook group posts:** Works occasionally, but no verification, no payment, no map, no reliability
- **Seafood restaurants/markets:** Middleman still exists; freshness is unknown

**What it costs them:**
- Watermen: 15–20% lower earnings on every pound sold through wholesale
- Buyers: Pay retail prices for product that may be days old, sourced from questionable chains

**Emotional tension:**
- Watermen: Pride in their craft, frustration at being undervalued, anxiety about the viability of the trade for the next generation
- Buyers: Nostalgia for a simpler, more connected relationship with food; distrust of industrial seafood supply chains

## Competitive Landscape

**Direct:** No direct competitor exists for dock-to-door Chesapeake Bay seafood discovery. Crabeezy is the first mover.

**Secondary — informal:** Facebook buy/sell group posts where watermen occasionally post availability. Falls short because: no payment, no verification, no map, no consistency.

**Secondary — CSA boxes/local seafood subscriptions:** Services like Dock to Dish or local seafood CSAs. Falls short because: subscription model, no real-time availability, limited to specific species/seasons.

**Indirect — grocery store seafood:** Convenient but inferior product, no connection to source, supports industrial supply chain.

**Indirect — existing seafood markets and dock retail:** Some watermen sell at the dock informally. Falls short because: buyers can't find them, no digital presence, cash only, inconsistent availability.

## Differentiation

**Key differentiators:**
- Live map of real-time haul availability — no other platform does this for local Chesapeake Bay seafood
- DNR commercial license verification on every seller — buyers know every listing is legal and legitimate
- Instant Stripe payouts for watermen — same-day money vs. waiting on wholesaler checks
- Built-in POS for walk-up dock sales — handles both pre-orders and in-person transactions in one screen
- Direct buyer-waterman relationship — the middleman is gone, not just reduced

**How we do it differently:** We are infrastructure, not inventory. We don't touch the product. We give watermen the tools to sell direct and give buyers a reliable discovery layer.

**Why that's better:** The product is fresher (caught today, sold today), the waterman earns more (retail pricing), and the buyer feels the connection of knowing exactly who caught their food and where.

**Why buyers choose us:** They can't get dock-fresh crabs any other way with this kind of reliability and ease.

**Why watermen choose us:** It's the first tool built specifically for them — not adapted from restaurant or food delivery software.

## Objections

| Objection | Response |
|-----------|----------|
| "I don't want to deal with technology" | Setup takes 10 minutes. If you can post on Facebook, you can use Crabeezy. We built it for watermen, not tech people. |
| "I already sell at the dock — I don't need this" | Crabeezy adds buyers you'd never find at the dock, handles pre-orders, and gives you a record of every sale. It doesn't replace your dock — it amplifies it. |
| "Is this legit? Can I actually trust this app?" | Every seller is verified through their DNR commercial license and health permits. Every transaction goes through Stripe, the same payment processor that powers millions of businesses. |
| "What if I don't catch anything that day?" | You control your listing. Only post when you have product. You can update availability in real time from your phone. |

**Anti-persona (who Crabeezy is NOT for):**
- Large commercial seafood distributors or wholesalers
- Recreational crabbers (no commercial license = can't sell)
- Buyers who want grocery-store convenience and don't care about sourcing

---

## Switching Dynamics

**Push (what drives watermen away from wholesalers):** Resentment at low prices, no transparency, being treated as a commodity supplier with no direct relationship to the customer.

**Pull (what attracts them to Crabeezy):** Set your own price, get paid the same day, build your own customer base, have something to show for a lifetime of work on the water.

**Habit (what keeps them stuck):** "This is how it's always been done." Existing buyer relationships with wholesalers. Reluctance to trust technology or deal with something unfamiliar.

**Anxiety (what worries them about switching):** "What if buyers don't show up?" "What if it doesn't work?" "I don't want to be stuck with unsold product."

## Customer Language

**How watermen describe the problem:**
- "The wholesaler sets the price and we just take it"
- "I never know what I'm going to get paid until the check comes"
- "I've been doing this 30 years and I have nothing to show for it"
- "People will pay good money for fresh crabs — I just can't get to them"

**How buyers describe the problem:**
- "I want fresh crabs but I don't know where to go"
- "My neighbor always knows a guy — I never know the guy"
- "The grocery store crabs are garbage"
- "I'd pay more if I knew they were actually fresh"

**Words to use:**
- Fresh (specific — "pulled this morning," "straight off the boat")
- Waterman (not "fisherman" — this is a Chesapeake Bay term of pride)
- Dock-to-dish
- Direct
- Verified
- Local (specific — county names, Bay reference)
- Chesapeake
- Your catch, your price

**Words to avoid:**
- Platform (use "app" or "marketplace")
- Disrupt / disrupting (sounds tech-bro, not authentic)
- Ecosystem
- Leverage
- Middlemen (use "wholesaler" when being specific — it's more concrete)
- Scalable

**Glossary:**
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Waterman | A person who makes their living on the water harvesting seafood — used specifically in the Chesapeake Bay region. A term of identity and pride. |
| DNR license | Maryland Department of Natural Resources commercial fishing/crabbing license — required to legally sell seafood commercially |
| Dock-to-dish | Seafood sold direct from the point of harvest to the consumer, with no intermediary |

## Brand Voice

**Tone:** Direct, proud, community-rooted. Not corporate. Sounds like someone from the Bay, not a startup pitch deck.

**Style:** Conversational. Short sentences. Tells specific stories with real people and places. Never generic.

**Personality:**
- Proud of the Chesapeake Bay tradition
- Straight-talking (no fluff)
- Hardworking (mirrors the waterman ethic)
- Community-first
- Quietly confident (doesn't need to shout)

**Off-limits:** Emojis used ironically, startup clichés, references to "disrupting" fishing, treating watermen as a marketing prop rather than the product's hero
